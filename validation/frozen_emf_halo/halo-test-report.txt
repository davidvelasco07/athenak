Frozen-EMF MOOD halo experiment — 14 October 2026

With retained high-order EMFs and local fallback replacements, the tested
two-revision ghost counts are FV4: 6, FV6: 8, FV8: 10.

The initial detection halo is one cell per revision. For the current FV_N
reconstruction and face-to-centre magnetic interpolation, the derived bound is

    initial detection halo = number of revisions
    nghost >= N + number of revisions

                       1 revision   2 revisions   3 revisions   4 revisions
    FV4                     5            6             7             8
    FV6                     7            8             9            10
    FV8                     9           10            11            12

All entries matched the large-block reference at every tested candidate cell
and flag, including the shrinking ghost halo, in the 2D sweep. The bounds for
two revisions also passed in 3D. This is a result for the isolated prototype,
not a change to the source worktree or production runs.

Why these numbers follow from the retained-EMF method

The high-order fluxes and EMFs are evaluated once per stage. The prototype
also computes complete PLM/DC fallback EMFs from immutable stage data. A cell
demotion changes only its adjacent face fluxes and the EMFs on edges bounding
those faces. Every other edge retains its original high-order EMF. The final
CT update uses these selected EMFs without calling the full corner composition
again. A revision can therefore influence neighboring candidates by at most
one cell in any coordinate direction.

FV_N has cell reconstruction radius r=N/2 and edge reach r+1. The stage-input
face-to-centre B interpolation reaches another r-1 cells beyond a cell's
face interval. Its combination with face/edge reconstruction gives an initial
input footprint of (r+1)+(r-1)=N cells beyond the candidate region. Adding the
revision halo gives N+revisions. The original constructor's two-revision
checks were 9/12/15 for FV4/FV6/FV8; this experiment supports 6/8/10 for the
changed method. This does not validate the old recomposition method's bounds.

Evidence

72 completed CPU runs used Kokkos bounds checking, double-precision output,
UCT-HLLD, RK3, periodic boundaries, and the field-loop problem with magnetic
amplitude 0.1 and a density ratio of 2. Reference and test runs both used the
same frozen-EMF method. Comparisons required exact equality, not a tolerance.

* 2D: 64x64 cells, one block versus four blocks, FV4/FV6/FV8, 1–4 revisions.
* 3D: 32x32x32 rotated loop, one block versus eight blocks, all three orders
  with two revisions.
* Eight-step RK3 runs: all three orders at their proposed two-revision counts.
* Four MPI ranks: FV8 in both 2D and 3D at nghost=10.
* All positive cases matched initial/final fields, final flags and div(B),
  and every saved candidate and flag in the detector halo exactly.
* Physical-boundary ghost comparisons used the same global coordinates in
  the reference; they were not wrapped onto a different periodic copy.

A forced single-cell FV8 repair at global cell (32,32) changed exactly nine
candidate cells: the surrounding 3x3 neighborhood. No candidate outside one
cell changed. This directly measures the revision's support while the base
order is FV8.

An adversarial test detector seeded one cell and then rejected every candidate
changed by previous repairs. With 1–4 revisions, the full detection halo
matched the reference exactly. Reducing that halo by one produced different
final solutions in all four cases (maximum absolute field differences about
4.6e-4 to 6.5e-4). This is a deliberately synthetic detector for testing the
dependency cone, not a physical stability test.

The ordinary FV4 loop also produced a different final solution when the
two-revision detection halo was shortened from two cells to one, even with
16 input ghost cells. Adequate input data cannot replace the revision halo.

Reducing nghost by one below N+revisions changed outer-halo candidates in the
2D/3D checks. For example, with two revisions FV4 at nghost=5 also changed
48 halo flags in the first stage's first detection pass. The ordinary loop's
final interior fields still matched at the one-cell-smaller ghost counts;
that alone would have missed the incorrect ghost candidates. Thus the counts
above certify exact tested halo computation, rather than claiming every
smaller count immediately destabilizes the physical solution.

After eight steps, max|div(B)| was 7.55e-15, 7.11e-15 and 6.44e-15 for
FV4/FV6/FV8, respectively, identical to the corresponding large-block runs.

Scope and saved artifacts

Baseline: 2d251bb09d67fe76ac97d419041c33dc906db2ca from fallback-validation.
The original worktree was read only. The experiment is in an isolated copy.

This is a reference implementation that caches full fallback tiers to make
the dependency test unambiguous. It is not optimized for production memory
or speed. SMR/AMR, GR/SR, well balance, diffusion, GPU execution, and nonperiodic
boundaries were not certified. In particular, these numbers should not yet
replace production checks for those configurations.

halo-test-results.json contains all run parameters, comparisons, source
hashes, and the executable hash. frozen-emf-prototype.patch contains the
experimental method; halo-audit-instrumentation.patch adds snapshots, forced
detectors, and double-precision binary output. Apply both to the recorded
baseline to obtain the tested source. The reproduction archive includes the
scripts and per-run inputs/logs, without the large raw snapshot arrays.
